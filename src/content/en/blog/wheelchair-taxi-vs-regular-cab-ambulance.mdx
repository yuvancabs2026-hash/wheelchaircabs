Choosing a ride for a wheelchair user is about more than finding a car. The vehicle needs to work for the passenger's mobility equipment, boarding needs, companions, and the purpose of the trip. A wheelchair taxi, a regular cab, and an ambulance each serve different needs.

## When is a wheelchair taxi a good choice?

A wheelchair-accessible taxi is designed for planned journeys when a passenger needs a vehicle that can accommodate wheelchair boarding. Depending on the vehicle, this may include a ramp or lift and a way to secure the wheelchair during travel. Ask the operator which boarding system is available and whether it suits your chair.

For stable passengers travelling to a doctor's appointment, dialysis session, physiotherapy, a hospital follow-up, or a family visit, an accessible cab can be more practical than trying to fit a wheelchair into a standard car. Share your wheelchair dimensions and weight, the number of people travelling, pickup and drop-off details, and whether you need a return trip before booking.

## Why might a regular cab not work?

Most ordinary taxis are not configured for a passenger to remain in a wheelchair during the ride. A passenger may need to transfer to a car seat, while someone folds and stores the wheelchair. This may be manageable for some people, but it can be uncomfortable or impractical for others—especially when using a powered chair or when transfers require assistance.

Before booking a standard cab, consider:

- Can the passenger transfer safely and comfortably into a vehicle seat?
- Is there enough boot space for the folded wheelchair and other mobility aids?
- Can a caregiver assist without lifting beyond what is safe for them?
- Does the route include a step-free pickup and drop-off point?

Never assume a vehicle is wheelchair accessible based only on the word "cab" or "accessible" in a listing. Ask about the exact vehicle and boarding arrangement.

## When should you arrange an ambulance instead?

An ambulance is the appropriate option when a passenger needs urgent medical attention, medical monitoring, oxygen, or clinical support while travelling. A wheelchair taxi is not an ambulance and does not provide medical care.

If you are unsure whether a passenger can travel safely in a regular accessible taxi, ask their healthcare provider. For emergencies, contact local emergency services rather than booking a taxi.

## Wheelchair cab for a hospital visit: what should you confirm?

When booking a hospital taxi or cab for a medical appointment, share:

1. Your exact pickup address and the hospital's requested entrance or drop-off point.
2. The appointment time and any time you need to allow for traffic and boarding.
3. Whether you use a manual or powered wheelchair and its measurements.
4. How many caregivers will travel and whether luggage or medical equipment is coming along.
5. Whether you need a return journey, a waiting period, or a recurring booking for dialysis.

Ask the service provider to confirm the vehicle can accommodate your wheelchair before finalizing the trip. For regular appointments, ask whether the same time slot can be requested for future rides.

## A simple rule of thumb

Choose a regular cab only when the passenger can safely transfer and the wheelchair can be carried. Choose a wheelchair taxi for a planned trip when the passenger needs an accessible vehicle and the specific vehicle has been confirmed as suitable. Arrange an ambulance when medical care or monitoring is required during travel.

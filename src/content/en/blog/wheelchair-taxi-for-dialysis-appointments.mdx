Recurring medical treatment can mean travelling to a clinic or hospital several times each week. For wheelchair users and caregivers, arranging a suitable ride is part of the treatment-day routine. Planning the pickup, boarding, return journey, and wheelchair fit in advance can make a dialysis or physiotherapy trip easier to coordinate.

## Is a wheelchair taxi suitable for dialysis travel?

A wheelchair taxi may suit a planned, non-emergency appointment when the passenger is stable and does not need clinical monitoring or medical treatment during the journey. It is not an ambulance and does not provide medical care. If a passenger needs oxygen, monitoring, or urgent support while travelling, ask the healthcare provider about appropriate medical transport.

Before booking, confirm that the particular accessible vehicle can accommodate the passenger's wheelchair. Wheelchair dimensions, weight, and configuration vary. Let the taxi operator know whether it is manual or powered and share measurements if requested.

## A checklist for booking recurring treatment rides

When you contact a wheelchair taxi service, share the following details:

- **Treatment schedule:** appointment dates and preferred pickup time.
- **Pickup and drop-off:** full address, building access information, and clinic or hospital entrance.
- **Wheelchair details:** type, approximate width and weight, and any special boarding needs.
- **Companion seating:** how many family members or caregivers will travel.
- **Return plan:** whether the driver should return later, wait, or make a separate pickup.
- **Contact person:** a phone number the driver or booking team can reach on the day.

Ask whether repeat bookings are available, how far ahead to reserve, and how the service handles schedule changes. Confirm the fare and any waiting charges in advance; do not assume the same trip always has the same price.

## Give yourself time for boarding and traffic

For a hospital cab booking, choose a pickup time that allows for traffic, boarding, hospital access, and check-in. If the appointment is early in the morning or falls during a busy period, enquire in advance. Share the exact drop-off location so the driver can plan for the right entrance.

If the hospital visit includes a return trip, ask how to request pickup when the appointment finishes. Treatment schedules can change, so agree on a clear way to communicate timing updates with the taxi team.

Keep essential items close at hand, including appointment information, contact details, and anything the passenger normally needs during travel. Let the driver know if the passenger needs extra time to board or a particular kind of assistance. Caregivers should avoid lifting a passenger or wheelchair in a way that feels unsafe.
